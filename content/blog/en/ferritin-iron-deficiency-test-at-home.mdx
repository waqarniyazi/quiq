## Ferritin vs Haemoglobin — Why Ferritin Is the Better Marker

Think of your body's iron like a bank account. Haemoglobin is the cash you use for daily expenses—it's the iron actively circulating and carrying oxygen. Ferritin, however, is your savings account. It’s the stored iron your body taps into when your daily intake isn't enough. When you start becoming deficient, you spend your savings (ferritin) long before you run out of cash (haemoglobin). This is why a ferritin test can reveal a deficiency months before a standard Complete Blood Count (CBC) test shows low haemoglobin and diagnoses anaemia.

Routine blood tests often focus on haemoglobin, completely missing this early depletion stage. You could be feeling persistently tired, weak, and foggy-brained with "normal" haemoglobin but critically low ferritin. By the time haemoglobin drops, the deficiency is advanced and harder to correct. Monitoring ferritin gives you a proactive window to address the root cause with diet or supplements, preventing the more serious health impacts of anaemia.

## Who Is Most at Risk in India

Certain groups are disproportionately affected by iron deficiency in India. Women, particularly those with heavy menstrual periods, are at the forefront, losing significant iron monthly. Pregnancy and breastfeeding further increase iron demands dramatically. Vegetarians and vegans also face a higher risk, as plant-based iron (non-heme iron) is not as easily absorbed by the body as the iron from animal sources (heme iron).

Other high-risk groups include athletes, whose bodies use more iron for oxygen transport and muscle function, and the elderly, who may have reduced dietary intake or absorption issues. Chronic conditions that affect nutrient absorption can also lead to deficiency. ## Symptoms That Get Dismissed as 'Normal'

The early signs of low iron stores are frustratingly vague and often brushed aside as just being "busy" or "stressed." Persistent fatigue that isn't relieved by sleep is a classic sign. You might experience unusual weakness, shortness of breath from minor exertion, or frequent headaches. Physical clues include pale skin, brittle nails, hair loss, and a sore or swollen tongue.

Perhaps the most insidious symptom is "brain fog"—difficulty concentrating, memory lapses, and a lack of mental clarity. These symptoms can severely impact your daily life and work performance. It’s easy to attribute them to a hectic lifestyle, but they are your body's direct signals that it's struggling to produce enough healthy red blood cells to oxygenate your tissues and brain effectively.

## Next Steps After Testing

A low or borderline result is a powerful piece of information for your health journey. It’s a signal to take action. The first step should be to consult with your doctor. They can help interpret your result in the context of your overall health and recommend an appropriate course, which may include iron supplements.
