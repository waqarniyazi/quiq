## What C-Reactive Protein (CRP) Measures

Think of C-Reactive Protein (CRP) as your body's primary smoke alarm. It's a protein produced by your liver in response to inflammation. When your immune system detects a threat—be it an injury, an infection like a bad cold, or the slow-burn stress of poor lifestyle choices—it sends out signals that tell your liver to release CRP into your bloodstream. A standard CRP test measures this general inflammation. There's also a more sensitive version, hs-CRP (high-sensitivity CRP), which can detect even low-grade, chronic inflammation that might otherwise go unnoticed.

This isn't about the temporary, helpful inflammation that helps a sprained ankle heal. We're talking about the chronic, systemic kind that simmers quietly for years. For millions of Indians navigating busy urban lives, with diets high in processed foods and increasing stress levels, this internal fire is often constantly stoked. Monitoring your CRP gives you a direct, quantifiable insight into this process, acting as an early warning system long before more serious symptoms or conditions develop.

## High CRP Links to Heart Disease, Diabetes, and Autoimmune Conditions

Elevated CRP levels are a significant red flag, strongly associated with an increased risk of major health issues. Research consistently shows that people with high CRP are more likely to develop cardiovascular diseases. The inflammation damages blood vessel walls, making it easier for plaque to build up.

Furthermore, chronic inflammation is a key player in insulin resistance, the precursor to Type 2 Diabetes—a condition affecting over 77 million adults in India. High CRP levels can indicate that your body is struggling with metabolic stress. It's also a common feature in autoimmune conditions like rheumatoid arthritis, where the body mistakenly attacks its own tissues. ## Who Should Test

While anyone health-conscious can benefit from knowing their inflammation status, certain groups should consider it a priority. If you've recovered from COVID-19 but continue to battle lingering fatigue, brain fog, or body aches ("Long COVID"), a CRP test can help assess if residual inflammation is contributing to your symptoms. Similarly, individuals with persistent, unexplained fatigue that isn't resolved by rest are prime candidates.

People with known risk factors for heart disease or diabetes, including a family history, high blood pressure, or being overweight, should also monitor their CRP. It provides a crucial piece of the health puzzle. Even if you feel generally well but want to take a proactive, preventive approach to your health—especially in your 30s and 40s—understanding your baseline inflammation level is a powerful step. It empowers you to make lifestyle changes *before* a problem becomes a diagnosis.

## How to Interpret Results and What to Do Next

Interpreting your CRP result is straightforward, but it should always be done in consultation with a healthcare professional. Generally, a result below 1.0 mg/L indicates low risk for cardiovascular inflammation, 1.0 to 3.0 mg/L indicates average risk, and above 3.0 mg/L indicates high risk. A very high result (above 10.0 mg/L) often suggests an active infection or other acute condition and requires immediate medical attention.

If your levels are elevated, don't panic. This is valuable information, not a verdict. The next step is to consult your doctor to investigate the underlying cause. In parallel, you can embrace powerful, evidence-based anti-inflammatory lifestyle changes. Focus on a whole-foods diet rich in antioxidants (think colourful vegetables and fruits), incorporate regular moderate exercise like brisk walking or yoga, prioritise quality sleep, and manage stress through meditation or mindfulness. These steps can significantly lower inflammation over time.
