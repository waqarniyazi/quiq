## What Troponin Is and Why It's the Gold Standard Cardiac Marker

When heart muscle cells are damaged, as happens during a heart attack, they release specific proteins into the bloodstream. The most sensitive and specific of these proteins is Troponin. Think of it as a precise biochemical distress signal from your heart. Unlike other markers, Troponin levels rise significantly only when there is injury to the heart muscle, making it the definitive "gold standard" for diagnosing a myocardial infarction (heart attack) in clinical settings worldwide. There are two main types: Troponin I and Troponin T. Measuring these levels provides a clear, objective snapshot of cardiac cell damage, far more reliable than symptoms alone, which can be vague and misleading.

## Symptoms That Often Get Dismissed

We’ve all seen the dramatic movie scene of a heart attack. Reality is often far less cinematic and more insidious. The symptoms can be so mild or unusual that people attribute them to indigestion, fatigue, or stress.

**Chest Tightness and Breathlessness:** This isn't always sharp pain. It can feel like uncomfortable pressure, squeezing, or a heavy weight on your chest. It may come and go. Similarly, sudden shortness of breath while doing routine tasks—like climbing a flight of stairs you manage daily—is a major red flag. You might find yourself gasping for air without any obvious exertion.

Other commonly ignored signs include discomfort in the arms, back, neck, jaw, or stomach. Unexplained nausea, breaking out in a cold sweat, or sudden, overwhelming fatigue are also critical warnings, especially for women, whose symptoms can be more atypical. In the Indian context, dismissing these as "gas," "body pain," or just the result of a long workday can have fatal consequences.
