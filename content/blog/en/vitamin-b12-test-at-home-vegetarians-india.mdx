## What Vitamin B12 Does for Your Body

Vitamin B12, or cobalamin, is a powerhouse nutrient essential for some of your body's most critical functions. Primarily, it plays a starring role in maintaining a healthy nervous system. It helps produce the protective myelin sheath around your nerves, ensuring efficient communication between your brain and the rest of your body. Without adequate B12, this insulation can degrade, leading to neurological symptoms.

Furthermore, B12 is indispensable for the formation of healthy red blood cells, preventing a type of anaemia that causes weakness and fatigue. It's also crucial for DNA synthesis, impacting everything from cell repair to overall energy production. Emerging research even links adequate B12 levels to better mood regulation and cognitive function, highlighting its importance for mental well-being alongside physical health.

## Why Vegetarians and Vegans Are Most at Risk

The root cause of this widespread deficiency is simple: Vitamin B12 is naturally found almost exclusively in animal products like meat, eggs, fish, and dairy. While some plant-based foods like nutritional yeast are fortified, the traditional Indian vegetarian diet often lacks consistent, reliable sources. This makes the 30-40% of Indians who follow a vegetarian diet exceptionally vulnerable.

The risk doesn't stop at diet alone. As we age, our body's ability to absorb B12 from food diminishes, putting the elderly at higher risk. Certain health conditions like celiac disease or chronic digestive issues can also impair absorption. Additionally, common medications such as metformin (for diabetes) and long-term acid reducers can interfere with how your body utilises this vital vitamin. This combination of dietary sources and absorption challenges creates a perfect storm for deficiency.

## Symptoms You're Probably Ignoring

B12 deficiency is a master of disguise. Its early signs are subtle and easily attributed to our busy, modern lives. Do you often feel an unexplained fatigue that coffee can't fix? Struggle with "brain fog," memory lapses, or difficulty concentrating? These could be early whispers from your body. More specific neurological symptoms include pins-and-needles sensations (paresthesia), numbness in the hands and feet, or even a sore, red tongue.

Left unchecked, these symptoms can progress. You might experience significant mood changes, such as increased depression or irritability, muscle weakness, and problems with balance. Since these signs overlap with many other conditions, getting a clear answer through a specific test is the first step toward addressing the real cause.
