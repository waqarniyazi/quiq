India has made significant strides in its fight against HIV/AIDS, yet the journey is far from over. According to the National AIDS Control Organization (NACO), India has an estimated 2.4 million people living with HIV. While the prevalence is declining, a critical challenge remains: a massive testing gap. Many individuals remain unaware of their status, often due to fear, misinformation, and the deep-seated social stigma associated with the virus. This lack of knowledge prevents timely access to life-saving treatment and fuels further transmission. Knowing your HIV status is the first and most responsible step towards managing your health, whether the result is positive or negative. It empowers you with information and opens the door to care and support.

## Why Stigma Prevents People from Walking Into Labs or Clinics

The fear of being seen, judged, or discriminated against is a powerful deterrent. In many communities across India, an HIV test is not viewed as a routine health check but as an indicator of specific behaviours, leading to unwarranted moral judgment. People worry about confidentiality breaches at local clinics, the possibility of meeting acquaintances, or the perceived shame of being associated with the disease. This stigma isn't just social; it can have real-world consequences on relationships, employment, and mental well-being.

For young adults, migrant workers, and even married couples wanting a routine check, the thought of walking into an Integrated Counselling and Testing Centre (ICTC) or a pathology lab can be daunting. The process itself—filling forms, interacting with staff, waiting for results—can feel exposing. This fear creates a dangerous cycle of avoidance, where individuals delay testing until symptoms appear, by which time the infection may have progressed. Overcoming this barrier requires a paradigm shift in how testing is accessed.

## Resources After a Positive Result — ICTC Centres, Free ART

A reactive self-test result can be overwhelming, but it's important to know that HIV is a manageable chronic condition with proper treatment. The next step is mandatory: visiting your nearest Integrated Counselling and Testing Centre (ICTC). These government centres provide free, confidential confirmatory testing and essential pre-and post-test counselling by trained professionals.

India's National AIDS Control Programme is a global success story in providing free Antiretroviral Therapy (ART). If confirmed positive, you will be linked to an ART centre where you can receive lifelong medication at no cost. These medicines suppress the virus to undetectable levels, allowing people to live long, healthy lives and preventing transmission to others. ## Normalising Regular HIV Testing as Responsible Health Behaviour

The ultimate goal is to integrate HIV testing into our broader health consciousness. Just as we get regular check-ups for diabetes or cholesterol, knowing one's HIV status should be seen as a standard pillar of wellness. Regular testing is recommended for sexually active adults, individuals with multiple partners, couples before marriage or planning a pregnancy, and anyone who may have had a potential exposure.

Breaking the stigma starts with changing the conversation from one of fear to one of responsibility and empowerment. When testing becomes a private, accessible, and normalised act, we can close the testing gap, ensure early treatment, and move closer to ending the epidemic.
